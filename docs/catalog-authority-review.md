## Gamma is a positive enumeration route

The official [market keyset endpoint](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/markets/list-markets-keyset-pagination) documents cursor pagination, a maximum page size of 100, and `closed=false` by default. The [event keyset endpoint](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/events/list-events-keyset-pagination) permits 500 per page and exposes `closed`, date/start-time bounds, `series_id` and recurrence filters. Follow every returned `next_cursor` as `after_cursor` until its documented omission. The docs say a cursor is present only when count equals the effective limit and omitted on the last page; this does not require a cursor on every full terminal page. Record page counts alongside the server's terminal signal for independent corroboration. Explicitly enumerate both closed states; omitting the market filter silently selects open markets. A 422 or 503 is failure, never an empty catalog. Both keyset references document a possible 503 when keyset pagination is not configured.

The documented offset [events](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/events/list-events) and [markets](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/markets/list-markets) routes provide a fallback for bounded historical slices. Pin sort/filter parameters, detect duplicate IDs, preserve actual page/terminal evidence and repeat the scan to expose changing membership. A page budget or API offset cap is not terminal closure. The legacy event route additionally exposes an `archived` filter, useful to check whether archived listings appear in the principal enumeration.

[Series listing](https://docs.polymarket.com/api-reference/series/list-series) supports pagination and `exclude_events=true`; the latter avoids unnecessary embedded event payloads during series discovery. [Discovery documentation](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/discover-markets) describes a series as recurring related events, not an unconditional daily cardinality guarantee. Enumerated series IDs and broad event/market scans offer independent target discovery; quote-derived IDs, guessed slugs, tag-only searches and an empirical `7 * 288` count cannot supply their own denominator.

## Boundary and exception meaning

[Market details](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-data/market-details) describes current listing state, nullable dates and indexed correspondence of labels with token IDs. It says a market can remain discoverable after closing and defines archived markets as read-only. Therefore archived does not mean historically nonexistent, and `closed` must not be imported as winning-outcome evidence. Neither this page nor the list schema establishes `startDate` as the opening of a five-minute contract interval.

A terminal end-date-filtered scan establishes the result set for that predicate. It can miss an otherwise relevant row whose date is null or changed. The practical reconciliation is broad historical date scans plus date-free enumeration of independently discovered target series, with explicit interval/identity validation. Retain ambiguous classifications, additional or duplicate contracts, inconsistent mappings and null-time candidates; resolve them using first-party identity evidence or leave the affected day uncertified. Every qualifying contract must be accounted for, including exceptions outside a convenient grid.

No deleted-listing tombstone API, immutable catalog revision ID, transactional `as_of` parameter or unconditional seven-asset schedule was found in these references. This is a bounded documentation finding, not proof that no other capability exists. A missing slug or filtered-list absence does not establish "never listed." Conversely, an independently closed first-party enumeration with positive accounting of the target contracts is materially stronger than the earlier native lifecycle-only proposal: it is not selected from the research observations. Freeze its identity projections, requests, page counts, hashes and observation times as a catalog generation; later first-party changes require a new generation. Permanent future immutability of Gamma is not a prerequisite for a versioned claim.

## On-chain identity and listing evidence

The official [contract registry](https://docs.polymarket.com/resources/contracts) identifies Polygon mainnet, chain 137, and CTF `0x4D97DCd97eC945f40cF65F87097ACe5EA0476045`. First-party [UMA adapter source](https://github.com/Polymarket/uma-ctf-adapter/blob/8b76cc9e0d46c6f7450a0adb0ddc0f5b0568c9cc/src/UmaCtfAdapter.sol) prepares a binary CTF condition and emits `QuestionInitialized` containing question ID, request timestamp, creator and ancillary metadata. Such creation evidence could recover a question's historical existence and identity despite a later API omission, without collecting its eventual result. Adapter [deployment releases](https://github.com/Polymarket/uma-ctf-adapter/releases) list several Polygon versions; inspecting one current address alone is not an exhaustive historical scan.

The old first-party [exchange registry](https://github.com/Polymarket/ctf-exchange/blob/main/src/exchange/mixins/Registry.sol) emits `TokenRegistered` for complementary tokens and condition ID. However, the current [exchange V2 source](https://github.com/Polymarket/ctf-exchange-v2/blob/ccc0596074f4dfd62c944fbca4de252893b82b4b/README.md#removed-features) explicitly removes token registration and permits valid CTF token IDs to trade directly. V1 registration therefore cannot be assumed to enumerate August 2026 listings.

An on-chain catalog remains an applicable investigative route, not a measured remedy or an exhausted capability. A defensible version would bind contract deployments/ABIs, creation-log topic filters, a complete relevant block range and pinned block hashes, then classify the ancillary identity metadata and derive/verify token mappings. Condition preparation alone is not evidence that the corresponding question was actually listed on the CLOB: [market concepts](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/markets-events) explicitly allows on-chain markets unavailable for order-book trading. Creation/registration evidence can resolve positive existence; using its absence to declare a listing exception requires complete applicable deployment and listing semantics. Do not read settlement, payout, trade or winning-result events to close this identity gate. No free first-party historical log transport or complete deployment coverage has yet been demonstrated by the project; no transport limitation is asserted without that check.

## Why creation-log acquisition alone does not replace listing evidence

The next route-access probe is still pending as this section is written. Its chain-ID request, if successful, would establish only basic RPC access, not historical log coverage or a listing catalog.

Under the inspected contract and platform semantics, two histories can have identical question-initialization and condition-preparation records but different off-chain listing histories: a prepared condition is published/enabled in one and never listed in the other. Preparation does not encode the CLOB `enableOrderBook` state, and exchange V2 no longer requires a token-registration event. These distinctions are supported by the linked [market concepts](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/markets-events) and [V2 changes](https://github.com/Polymarket/ctf-exchange-v2/blob/ccc0596074f4dfd62c944fbca4de252893b82b4b/README.md#removed-features), not by an assumption that unseen quote continuity is necessary.

Additional creation records can discover previously unknown condition identities, recover ancillary question text and expose more candidates to reconcile. Their usefulness is not limited to rechecking known markets. Nevertheless, creation records alone cannot determine the missing off-chain listing fact or justify a "never listed" exception. They would need a complete applicable listing rule/registry, or individual positive listing evidence for every relevant prepared candidate plus complete deployment, block-range and classification coverage. Defining the expected universe as all prepared conditions would be a separate universe change.

Consequently, a broad creation-log download is not by itself a remedy for a confirmed inability to access the historical listing catalogs. If the bounded follow-up confirms catalog-route denial, the concrete continuation condition is ordinary authorized access from the required free GitHub Linux runners to an applicable first-party catalog, or another accessible first-party historical listing enumeration that can be closed and reconciled, including identity, both token mappings, interval membership and exceptions. This is a catalog access/evidence condition, not a claim that PendulumFlow V3 or the accepted OBSERVED representation is fundamentally incapable. No transport is called exhausted before the actual access results are inspected.
